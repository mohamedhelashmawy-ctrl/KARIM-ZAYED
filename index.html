The Photographer

I grew up between two silences — the considered quietude of a Scandinavian winter and the dense, humming stillness you find on a Tokyo side-street at three in the morning. Neither place ever felt loud to me. What felt loud was the space between people: the pause before a word is spoken, the moment a stranger's gaze passes through you on a crowded platform.

My practice is rooted in that in-between space. I shoot on 35mm film, almost always available light, almost always alone. The grain is not nostalgia — it is honesty. Digital smoothness flatters; grain tells the truth about how a moment actually felt. I am drawn to the wabi-sabi principle that impermanence and imperfection are not flaws to be corrected but the very texture of what is real.

Trained formally in Tokyo, I have spent eight years carrying a camera through twenty-two countries, showing work in Osaka, Berlin, and beyond. My photographs have appeared in Foam, Aperture, and Vogue Japan. But the work that matters most to me is still the work nobody has seen yet — the contact sheet sitting in a tray at midnight, waiting.
